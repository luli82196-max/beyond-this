# Beyond This — Portfolio Presentation Guide v0.1

Date: 2026-08-25

## Project positioning

**Short version**  
《不止于此》是一件关于跨领域学习，以及观看与创造如何彼此发生的五幕浏览器交互作品。

**Expanded version**  
The audience begins with a seed and moves through Forest, Tree, Room, and Light. The experience turns a personal learning process into an explorable spatial narrative: observation becomes participation, and different media meet inside one continuous browser experience.

## Recommended viewing flow

1. Open the public URL full-screen with sound available but not required.
2. Let the first frame establish the title and positioning; do not pre-explain the controls.
3. Move continuously through Seed, Forest, and Tree to demonstrate the narrative and visual transitions.
4. Pause in Room. Show how Book, Process, and Projection express one project through different media without leaving the experience.
5. Finish at Light, allow the completion state to settle, then open “About” for the concise project, method, and technology summary.

## Three-minute introduction

**0:00–0:25 — Premise**  
“Beyond This is a five-act interactive work about cross-disciplinary learning and the relationship between seeing and making. I wanted the portfolio itself to behave like a work, rather than a list of project cards.”

**0:25–1:15 — Experience**  
Move through Seed, Forest, and Tree. Explain that scroll/touch is treated as authored time: the audience controls pace while the sequence, transitions, sound, and visual focus remain composed.

**1:15–2:20 — Room and BT-P03**  
Pause in Room. Present Book, Process, and Projection as three views of the same work. Emphasize the separation of content, presentation state, interaction, and media lifecycle, which lets each medium remain coherent without becoming a separate page or app.

**2:20–3:00 — Outcome**  
Complete Light and open About. Summarize the browser-native delivery, responsive input, accessibility fallbacks, and performance-aware media handling. End with what the project demonstrates: creative direction, interaction design, real-time graphics, and production engineering working as one system.

## Interview talking points

- **Creative direction:** a five-act progression converts an abstract learning theme into a spatial and temporal experience.
- **Interaction design:** one continuous gesture keeps entry simple; authored thresholds preserve rhythm and meaning.
- **System design:** chapter loading, controller state, and presentation adapters keep narrative logic separate from rendering and content.
- **Real-time graphics:** Three.js/WebGL scenes use procedural and authored assets while maintaining continuity between chapters.
- **Content architecture:** BT-P03 Book, Process, and Projection share a registry but retain medium-specific presentation behavior.
- **Robustness:** strict TypeScript, regression harnesses, reduced-motion support, static fallback, lazy chapter preparation, and explicit media lifecycle boundaries.

## Audience hierarchy

- First 10 seconds: title, interactive-work category, theme, and one input instruction.
- First minute: authored visual identity and five-act direction.
- Room: concrete project depth and process evidence for mentors or interviewers.
- Light/About: concise form, method, technology, and approved author destinations.

Do not add résumé language, skill logos, awards, clients, or collaborators to the experience unless the information is approved and materially supports the work.
